The belt path

Belts are earned slowly on purpose. Most people take eight to twelve years to reach black. Open a belt to see what it means.

You learn the basics: how to escape, how to hold positions and how to tap safely. The goal is to survive and stay relaxed. There is no minimum time, and most students stay about two years.

You know the core techniques and can defend yourself against most opponents. This is where many people quit, so the real task is to keep showing up.

You build your own game: the few positions and moves that work for your body. Purple belts often start helping to teach.

You sharpen every detail and fix small leaks in your game. It is the last step before black.

Black belt is not the end, it is a license to keep learning. Degrees are added over decades, and the very top ranks are coral and red.

Stripes show progress between belts

Coaches add up to four white stripes on the black bar at the end of a belt. Four stripes means you are close to your next belt, but the coach decides when you are ready.
